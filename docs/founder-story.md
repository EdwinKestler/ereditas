# Founder story — why Ereditas exists

Source: Edwin Kestler, 4 Nov 2026 (his words, condensed for judges).
Use on the site, in the 3-minute video, and in the submission summary.
Do not embellish facts beyond this file.

## Facts (as told)

- In 2008 Edwin's father died suddenly.
- To notify his father's clients and providers, Edwin had to break into his
  phone for messages and contacts.
- He had to get into his father's email to cancel subscriptions and reach
  important providers and contacts.
- Presentations, documents, photos and other digital assets sat behind
  passwords nobody had.
- Formal legal succession is an analog process laid over digital assets; it
  took (and still takes) two to five years. No service existed to speed the
  digital part up.
- Insight: access to digital assets is key management, and key management
  can be programmable. In 2008 that was out of reach. IoT, telemetry and
  agents make it buildable now.

## Short quote (site, ~70 words)

> In 2008 my father died suddenly. To tell his clients what had happened, I
> had to break into his phone. To cancel his subscriptions and reach the
> people who mattered, I had to get into his email. His documents and photos
> sat behind passwords no one had. The legal succession took years; his
> digital life couldn't wait. Back then, automating that was impossible.
> Today, it isn't.
> — Edwin Kestler, builder

## Why Meta (one paragraph)

Life is announced and answered inside Meta's apps. Memorial posts appear on
Facebook and Instagram; the people who can confirm what happened answer on
WhatsApp. Ereditas reads those signals with SAM 3.1, asks real people over
WhatsApp, and uses Muse Spark to write to the heirs. Meta's apps are what
make a human-confirmed digital succession feasible.

## Values to name

Confidentiality · security · transparency · delivery.

## Thesis line

AI agents that carry out your will when you no longer can — but only after
the people you trust confirm it.
